Two sides of the same technology

AI isn't good or bad by itself — it depends on how, and on what, it's built and used.

Where it genuinely helps

  • Early disease detection in medical scans
  • Real-time translation across languages
  • Accessibility tools — screen readers, voice typing, captioning
  • Personalized tutoring and adaptive learning
  • Faster disaster prediction and climate modelling

Where it can go wrong

  • Bias — unfair outcomes when training data reflects real-world inequity
  • Misinformation — convincing fake text, images, and video (deepfakes)
  • Job displacement in roles built around repetitive tasks
  • Privacy erosion from large-scale data collection
  • Over-trust — accepting AI output without checking it

Why this balance matters

Most real AI harms aren't dramatic sci-fi scenarios — they're quieter: a resume-screening tool that was never tested on diverse resumes, a recommendation system that nudges people toward outrage because it drives engagement, a student submitting AI-written work they don't actually understand. Awareness is mostly about asking "where did this answer come from, and should I trust it?" before acting on it.
